How expected is built
  • Data: TfL’s live crowding index for every station, trains with a live arrival prediction for every line, line status and station closures, collected every 15 minutes and built into processed/moments.json by code/build.py each day.
  • Expected crowding is the same “usual” as Plot 6: each station’s median for the same quarter-hour (plus or minus 15 minutes) on other days of the same type (Monday to Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday), leaving the day itself out. It needs two other days of the type, so early Fridays and weekends have none.
  • Expected trains is the usual for that hour and day type, with disrupted hours left out, as in Plot 6.
  • Closed, exit-only and fully suspended stations are left out of crowding, exactly as in every other plot. Stations that never report show as dashed dots.
  • Line and network values are the mean across their stations, for both observed and expected. Each chart is scaled so 100 is the busiest average quarter-hour of the week in the chosen scope.
  • The grey band is expected plus or minus two normal spreads, a robust estimate (1.4826 × median absolute deviation) of how far observed usually strays from expected, separately for the morning peak, midday, evening peak and other times.
  • The routes come from TfL’s route sequences (raw/reference/routes.json, fetched by code/fetch_routes.py). Contains TfL data powered by TfL Open Data.
How the checks are decided
  • Fewer trains: checked line by line. A line counts when it runs at least 10% below its expected trains, and below its expected range, for at least two quarter-hours around the strain. The trains panel in the chart is the scope total, so one line’s loss looks small at network scale.
  • Disruption logged: for one line, any TfL status other than good on that line overlapping the strain window. For the whole network, only severe delays or worse count, because on ten lines there is almost always a minor delay somewhere.
  • Elsewhere on the network: for a line or station scope, any other line with a sustained fall in trains or a severe disruption (severe delays or worse) logged in the same window.
  • How widespread: the share of stations in scope above their own expected range at the peak. Concentrated means 15% or fewer stations, or three stations giving at least half the excess. Widespread means 40% or more.
